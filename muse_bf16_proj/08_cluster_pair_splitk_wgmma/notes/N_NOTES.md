## iteration 2 — bt (W as B, 8-row tiles, 128 CTAs x 1024: uses 128 SMs for the slice) and ta (G's tc generalised)

tcm 2.82-3.07; o m=1 ta(8 warps) 3.78-4.0 vs G tc 3.81-4.08; o m=8 ta 4.35-4.6.

- Ablations (bt slice, 80 reps): no escapes -0.2 (m=1) / -0.4 (m=8); no MMA -0.1; no x loads ~0; all three

## iteration 3 — timelines (tr_nk.py, clock64 per warp, judge-style replays)
bt slice m=1 (per CTA, us after CTA start; clock measured 1.76 GHz with HMMA vs 1.97 without — HMMA lowers the SM
clock 11 %): warp's first W 0.9-1.9 (p10-p90), CTA's last W 2.0-2.26, barrier +0.43, end +0.27 => 0.7 us tail.
ta o m=1 (512x256): last W p50 2.85, barrier +0.43, end +0.27; ta o m=8 same shape of tail.
Decomposition of the 0.7 us tail: x lands after the warp's W (+0.07: x is issued after W and queues behind the SM's W
flood), decode+HMMA of the last warps +0.2 (HMMA throughput burst at the end + 1.75 GHz), escapes/barrier skew
+0.1-0.2, final smem reduction + store 0.27 (escape slots read with 16-way bank conflicts: fixed in iteration 4).
CTA-to-CTA end skew p10->p90 0.4-0.5 us (kernel time = slowest CTA).

## iteration 4 — tail fixes (each measured same-run; most are dead ends)
- Escape slot smem layout [tok][e][row] (conflict-free): -0.05..-0.1 on the reduction phase.
- Shuffle-tree final reduction over all warps: +0.2..+1.2 us (!) vs 2-4 threads summing serially -> dead end
  (bar->end 0.27 -> 0.7-1.05 us in traces); 4-accumulator serial sum (RED4): +-0.05.
- Dedicated escape warp (slots first, gathers off the MMA warps): +-0.1, no gain (its gathers queue behind W too).
- Named barrier between W load batches (interleave issue across warps): no gain.
- x-warp staging x in smem (sp2.py: cp.async or cp.async.bulk + barrier): +0.2..+2 us vs every warp loading its own
  x after its W -> dead end. The x read has no faster path: it needs the param (cold, 0.3-0.5 us) and then queues
  behind the W flood whatever unit issues it.
- Exact escape scheme (new): out = (sum x*fake + sum_esc(-x*2^-15)) * 2^(E_lo-112) + sum_esc x*true: the fake 2^-15
  contribution cancels exactly and the true product is added after the power-of-two scale -> bit-exact one-hot rows
  for every injected value (G's delta form flushes values < 2^-39); dense rel err unchanged (1.6e-3).

## iteration 5 — router m=1 (rg.py: alygg77's rgemv structure, NVRTC, variants)
- W load flavour matters for the FP32 stream: `ld.global.nc.L1::no_allocate` +0.35..+0.45 us vs `ld.global.nc` /
  `__ldg` (3.04-3.14 vs 2.67-2.75, same run). For the 12-bit kernels the flavour is neutral (+-0.05).
- Baked W pointer: 2.69 vs 2.67 (no gain); 2 rows/CTA, 512/1024 threads, x first: all >= rgemv. rg == rgemv (2.67-2.78
  vs 2.69-2.78): nothing beats bo6 at m=1 with FP32 bytes.
- 24-bit hi/lo (6.3 MB) at m=1 not built: ~7 ALU/weight FMA decode (0.43 us issue per SMSP) lands partly in the tail,
  and the 24-bit escapes cannot be made bit-exact cheaply (escape weights never decode to 0 in the SIMD byte decode;
  a masking step costs ~1.2 op/weight). Estimated net <= -0.15 us; not attempted.

- nsys in-graph (nsys_shapes.py, 20 reps): nk_o1 4.58 vs bo6 g1_o_tc 4.70; nk_o8 5.06 vs bo6 v18s_gemv 5.06.
- ncu (ncu_n.sh, flushed, single launch): nk_o1 5.66 us, DRAM 12.7 MB = 47 % of 4.8 TB/s, L2 51 %, SM 20 %, issue
  29 %, hmma 6 %, occupancy 62 %, eligible 0.87 warps/clk, 7350 warp-inst/SM, 512x512 / 38 regs; stalls long_scoreboard
  50 %, barrier 17 %, not_selected 5 %. nk_o8 5.92 us, DRAM 12.7 MB = 45 %, issue 34 %, hmma 5 %, occ 69 %, elig 0.89,
  9016 warp-inst/SM, 38 regs; long_scoreboard 46 %, barrier 17 %. (bo6: g1_o_tc 5.63 / issue 22 % / occ 39 %;
  v18s 6.05 / issue 24 % / occ 46 %.)
- SASS (NVRTC 13.0, sass.py): nk_o1 38 regs, 0 spill, STACK 0, 264 instr, 7 LDG, 1 after the first HMMA (the escape
  x-gather, intended); nk_o8 39 regs, 0 spill, 328 instr, 14 LDG, 8 after the first HMMA (the 8 escape gathers,
  intended; all W/x/slot loads before it). Per warp: 3 W LDG.128 (2 low-byte + 1 nibble), 2 pairs x (2 decode12f =
  2 LOP3 + 1 SHF + 4 PRMT each, 2 HMMA) = 4 HMMA (m=1 and m=8 alike, n8 token tile), then 2 STS + barrier + sum.
- Binding unit: DRAM stream latency, not bandwidth or tensor: 45-47 % of DRAM peak averaged over the kernel
  (in-graph ~3.0-3.4 TB/s over 4.6-5.1 us vs 4.56 TB/s sustained), tensor pipe 5-6 %, issue 29-34 %. Why ta16 wins:
  twice the warps per SM (62 vs 32, occupancy 62-69 % vs 39-46 %) with half the bytes per warp => the last-landing
  warp's decode+HMMA+reduction tail is ~half, and compile-time M removes the runtime token loops/divisions. Remaining
  gap to the pure-stream probe (~3.1-3.4 us): x-after-W queueing + the CTA barrier/reduction tail (~0.3-0.4 us) and
  CTA end skew (~0.4 us p10->p90). Next lever would have to remove the per-CTA barrier tail (no idea found that
  survives the x-latency constraint) or reduce bytes (11-bit F11 costs ~1.3 % escapes in the tail, G measured +).
- At m=16 the same kernel (NT8 = 2) is +0.8..+1.2 us vs bo6's sk3: one-wave register tiles do not scale past m=8 for o.

## Dead ends / measured facts (summary)
- Every small-m kernel here (bo6's and mine) sits 0.3-0.9 us above a same-geometry pure-stream probe; the excess is a
  serial tail after the last W lands: x (issued after W, queued behind the SM's W flood), decode+HMMA burst at
  1.75 GHz, escape gathers, CTA barrier, smem reduction, store, plus 0.4-0.5 us CTA-to-CTA end skew.
- More SMs (128 vs 64 for the slice), fewer x readers (128 vs 512 CTAs for o), x-warps, bulk-copied x, escape warps,
  issue interleaving, tree reductions: none beat the incumbents.
- Per-graph placement noise is ~+-0.1 us at these sizes: single-graph A/Bs below 0.1 us are not decisions.
